How the calculation works

Net profit is your pay plus tips and bonuses, minus all your costs. Profit per hour is your net profit divided by the hours you worked.

Use the full time you spent, not only the time with a passenger or an order. Waiting for the next job and driving between jobs are part of the shift, and they are the reason a high-paying shift can still earn little per hour.

Costs to include

  • Fuel or charging.
  • Tolls and parking.
  • Food and drinks you bought during the shift.
  • Car wash, small repairs and phone data.

Wear on your vehicle is also a real cost. It is harder to measure, so many drivers add a rough amount per shift to “Other costs”. This calculator gives an estimate and is not tax advice. Tax rules differ by country, so ask a tax professional what you can claim.

Common questions

What is a good profit per hour for a driver?

It depends on your city, your vehicle and the hours you work, so no single number fits everyone. The useful comparison is your own: calculate it for each shift and see which days, hours and apps give you more per hour.

Should I include tips?

Yes. Tips are income you earned during the shift, so add them in the tips field.

Why is my profit per hour lower than the app showed me?

Gig apps usually show pay for the time with an order. This calculator divides your profit by all the hours you worked and also takes your costs out of your pay.
